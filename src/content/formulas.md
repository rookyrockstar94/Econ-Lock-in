## f-rules | How to answer calculation questions | SL | The habits that secure method marks.

### calc-rules | Calculation habits
@terms
Percentage change :: (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100.
Percentage point :: The simple difference between two percentages, e.g. an interest rate rising from 2% to 5% is a rise of 3 percentage points (but a 150% increase).
Index number :: A value expressed relative to a base period set at 100, used to show changes over time.
@end
1. Write the **formula**, then **substitute**, then the **answer** with **units**.
2. Round final answers to **2 decimal places** (unless told otherwise); do not round in the middle.
3. Keep the **sign**: PED is negative; a fall in GDP is negative growth; a deficit is negative.
4. Elasticities have **no units** and are never percentages.
5. Distinguish **percentage change** from **percentage points**.
6. Reuse earlier answers: examiners award follow-through marks.

:::formula Core tools
%Δ = \frac{new − old}{old} × 100
Index = \frac{value in year}{value in base year} × 100 :: base year = 100
Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height :: welfare losses, surplus
Area of a rectangle = base × height :: revenue, tax revenue, expenditure
:::

## f-micro | Microeconomics formulas | SL | Elasticities, linear functions, taxes, subsidies, price controls, surplus, costs and revenues.

### f-elasticity | Elasticities
@terms
@end
:::formula Elasticities
PED = \frac{%ΔQd}{%ΔP} :: |PED| > 1 elastic; < 1 inelastic
YED = \frac{%ΔQd}{%ΔY} :: > 1 luxury; 0–1 necessity; < 0 inferior
PES = \frac{%ΔQs}{%ΔP} :: > 1 elastic; < 1 inelastic
%ΔQd = PED × %ΔP :: rearranged to predict quantity change
:::
:::calc Elasticity examples
Price rises 8%, Qd falls 12% → PED = −12 ÷ 8 = **−1.5** (elastic).
PED = −0.5, price falls 10% → %ΔQd = −0.5 × −10 = **+5%**.
Income rises 4%, demand rises 10% → YED = **2.5** (luxury).
:::

### f-linear | Linear demand and supply functions | HL
@terms
@end
:::formula Linear functions
Qd = a − bP :: a = Q-intercept, b = change in Qd per $1
Qs = c + dP :: c = Q-intercept (may be negative), d = change in Qs per $1
Equilibrium: a − bP = c + dP → P = \frac{a − c}{b + d}
Price intercept of demand = \frac{a}{b}
With a specific tax t: Qs = c + d(P − t) :: P is the price consumers pay
With a subsidy s: Qs = c + d(P + s)
:::
:::calc Tax with functions
Qd = 100 − 5P, Qs = −20 + 5P → P = $12, Q = 40.
Tax of $2: Qs = −20 + 5(P − 2) = −30 + 5P → 100 − 5P = −30 + 5P → **Pc = $13**, Q = 35, **Pp = $11**.
Revenue = 2 × 35 = **$70**; consumer burden = (13 − 12) × 35 = $35; producer burden = (12 − 11) × 35 = $35; welfare loss = ½ × 2 × (40 − 35) = **$5**.
:::

### f-intervention | Taxes, subsidies, price controls and surplus | HL
@terms
@end
:::formula Intervention
Tax revenue = tax per unit × new quantity
Consumer burden = (Pc − P₁) × Q₂
Producer burden = (P₁ − Pp) × Q₂
Subsidy cost = subsidy per unit × new quantity
Shortage (ceiling) = Qd − Qs at Pmax
Surplus (floor) = Qs − Qd at Pmin; cost of buying it = Pmin × (Qs − Qd)
Consumer surplus = ½ × (price intercept of demand − P) × Q
Producer surplus = ½ × (P − price intercept of supply) × Q
Welfare loss (triangle) = ½ × (vertical gap) × (change in quantity)
Consumer expenditure = P × Q :: producer revenue = price received × Q
:::

### f-firm | Costs, revenue and profit | HL
@terms
@end
:::formula Theory of the firm
TC = TFC + TVC
ATC = \frac{TC}{Q} :: = AFC + AVC
AFC = \frac{TFC}{Q}
AVC = \frac{TVC}{Q}
MC = \frac{ΔTC}{ΔQ}
TR = P × Q
AR = \frac{TR}{Q} = P
MR = \frac{ΔTR}{ΔQ}
Profit = TR − TC = (AR − ATC) × Q
Profit-maximising output: MC = MR :: revenue-maximising output: MR = 0
Concentration ratio CRn = combined market share of the n largest firms
:::
:::calc Profit
P = $40, Q = 500, ATC = $34 → profit = (40 − 34) × 500 = **$3,000** (abnormal profit).
:::

## f-macro | Macroeconomics formulas | SL | National income, growth, inflation, unemployment, interest rates, the budget, the multiplier and tax rates.

### f-national-income | National income and growth
@terms
@end
:::formula National income
GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)
GNI = GDP + net income from abroad
Real GDP = \frac{nominal GDP}{GDP deflator} × 100
GDP per capita = \frac{GDP}{population}
Growth rate = \frac{real GDP_{t} − real GDP_{t−1}}{real GDP_{t−1}} × 100
:::

### f-prices-jobs | Inflation, unemployment and interest rates
@terms
@end
:::formula Prices, jobs, interest
Inflation rate = \frac{CPI_{t} − CPI_{t−1}}{CPI_{t−1}} × 100
Weighted price index = Σ (weight × item index) :: weights sum to 1 (HL)
Unemployment rate = \frac{unemployed}{labour force} × 100
Labour force = employed + unemployed
Real interest rate = nominal interest rate − inflation rate
Money multiplier (maximum) = \frac{1}{reserve ratio}
:::
:::calc Weighted index (HL)
Weights 0.5, 0.3, 0.2; item price changes +6%, +2%, −1% → weighted change = 3 + 0.6 − 0.2 = **+3.4%** → index 103.4.
:::

### f-fiscal | The budget, debt, the multiplier and tax rates
@terms
@end
:::formula Fiscal
Budget balance = tax revenue − government spending :: negative = deficit
Debt-to-GDP ratio = \frac{government debt}{GDP} × 100
Multiplier k = \frac{1}{1 − MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS + MPT + MPM} :: HL
ΔY = k × Δinjection :: HL
Required injection = \frac{output gap}{k} :: HL
Average tax rate = \frac{total tax}{total income} × 100 :: HL
Marginal tax rate = tax on the last dollar of income :: HL
:::

## f-global | Global economy formulas | SL | Exchange rates, the balance of payments, comparative advantage and trade protection.

### f-trade | Exchange rates, BOP and trade
@terms
@end
:::formula Global
Price in foreign currency = domestic price × exchange rate :: (foreign currency per unit of domestic)
%Δ in exchange rate = \frac{new − old}{old} × 100
Current account = goods balance + services balance + primary income + secondary income
Current account + capital account + financial account (+ errors) = 0
Opportunity cost of 1 X = \frac{units of Y given up}{units of X gained} :: HL comparative advantage
Tariff revenue = tariff per unit × imports after the tariff :: HL
Imports = Qd − Qs (domestic) at the domestic price
Terms of trade = \frac{index of export prices}{index of import prices} × 100
:::
:::calc Exchange rate
£1 = US$1.25. A $50 US textbook costs 50 ÷ 1.25 = **£40** in the UK. If the pound depreciates to £1 = $1.10, it costs 50 ÷ 1.10 = **£45.45**.
:::
