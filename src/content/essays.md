### p1b-carbon | Using real-world examples, evaluate the effectiveness of a carbon tax in reducing the negative externalities of fossil fuel use. | P1b | 15 | t2-8
- Theory: negative production externality diagram; carbon tax shifts MPC to MSC; Qm → Qopt.
- Example: Sweden's carbon tax (since 1991, ~US$120/tonne): emissions down ~30% while GDP grew; also British Columbia (2008) revenue-neutral tax.
- For: market-based, polluter pays, incentive to innovate, revenue can be recycled.
- Against: measurement of external cost; inelastic demand for energy; regressive; carbon leakage; political resistance (France gilets jaunes 2018).
- Alternatives: tradable permits (EU ETS) give certainty over quantity; regulation.
- Judgement: effective if set high enough, phased in predictably, revenue used to compensate low-income households, and coordinated internationally (border carbon adjustments such as the EU CBAM).

### p1b-subsidy | Using real-world examples, evaluate the use of subsidies to correct the underconsumption of merit goods. | P1b | 15 | t2-8

### p1b-monetary | Using real-world examples, evaluate the effectiveness of monetary policy in reducing inflation. | P1b | 15 | t3-5
